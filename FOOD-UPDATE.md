# Food logging update — 18 October 2026

Food now follows one flow: search, select, adjust the portion, and log. It automatically displays calories, protein, carbs and fat. No recipe builder or calorie-entry form is offered in the normal logging flow. Earlier manual entries remain readable; existing saved foods are preserved.

Individual ingredients can be searched alongside whole dishes or through the Ingredients shortcut. Common Indian names and dal phrases resolve to named reference foods. Raw/cooked preparation stays explicit. Familiar foods have simpler display names with original descriptions and source links available in the source disclosure.

The logger uses USDA records with all four main nutrition values, 51 original Indian dish estimates derived from source ingredients and fixed example cooked yields, two product-specific manufacturer records, saved foods, and the existing Open Food Facts barcode lookup. It excludes unresolved dishes rather than inventing values. The 255-name discovery index is not represented as 255 measured food profiles or an exhaustive database.

Reference servings show their gram/ml weights. A medium banana defaults to 118 g and a large hard-boiled egg to 50 g from USDA portions. Indian serving weights are stated app references, not universal household measurements. Portion changes scale unrounded source values; rounding is for display only. Unknown nutrients remain unknown. Manufacturer data must match the particular pack; homemade dish estimates cannot guarantee the exact nutrients on someone's plate.

MyFitnessPal data is not imported. See DATA-LICENSES.md for sources and permissions.

Validated: doubling/halving, grams and ml, source portion selection, invalid quantities, zero versus missing values, unresolved profiles, Indian aliases (including avoiding unrelated substring matches), saving, editing, updated daily totals and persistence after refresh. Browser checks used an isolated demo origin, preserving the owner's real local data. A 320px phone-width layout was checked as a preliminary responsive test; this is not real-device certification.
